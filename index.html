Progression & Meta

19 Achievements

Spanning combat, survival and story, each with a hint instead of a bare "???" (skeleton hunters get Rattled, Bone Collector and Skeleton Key; the toughest runs earn Half Century, Slaughterhouse and Unstoppable; buying upgrades earns Well Equipped and Maxed Out) — plus search, category filter, sort, and a Print list export in the Achievements panel.

Rank & Level System

A Rank Roadmap on the Profile panel showing every rank and the lifetime kills it takes.

Career Stats & Profile

Lifetime totals, a stats-over-time chart, a pinnable highlight reel, a Best Run breakdown, an anniversary line, today's session stats, and a "Nearly There" panel toward trackable achievements - plus a Bio others can see and private Notes only you can see.

Goals

Pick up to 3 personal goals to track/checklist on your Profile.

Upgrades & Points

Every Map 1 run earns Points (the blue star next to your coins) from its kills and waves. Spend them in Upgrades' Zombie Survival tab on boosts that last for every run after: more health and armor, more gun damage and headshot damage, faster reloads, more speed, starting coins, more coins from kills, more spare ammo, softer zombie hits, slow healing (Second Wind), a cheaper camp Trader and faster healing in the camp, with third picks after many of them (Kevlar Vest, Berserker, Gold Rush, Haggler and more) - 26 in all. Once you've survived to wave ten, Prestige trades your Points and Upgrades for 10% more Points from every run, per level - and each level costs more: 100,000 Points for the first, 200,000 for the second, and so on.

Quests

Rolling quests that come and go (kill zombies, reach a wave, finish runs), counted from your Map 1 runs and claimed for coins in the Quests panel.

World & Exploration

Build Mode / Map Editor

A standalone creative sandbox: hundreds of pixel-art blocks (every one with a slab and stairs), water and lava that flow (swim in water, jump off the bottom and climb out onto a ledge), free-fly flight, save slots plus the ready-made Map 1 city, Try Map to walk it in first person with a gun (or third person from behind or in front - the camera key switches), Export/Import Map, Mirror mode, a Line tool, and Copy/Paste for stamping down builds.

Open World

Map 1's city isn't the edge of the world any more: its roads run out through open gates as dirt tracks into an open world that goes on forever, with no edge in any direction. The land grows around you as you go, with 26 biomes from all over the Earth - oceans (deep, frozen, and warm ones with coral), beaches, rivers, plains, oak, birch, dark and cherry forests, taiga, snowy tundra and ice spikes, deserts with cacti, savanna, badlands mesas, jungles and bamboo, swamps and mangroves, mushroom fields, and mountains with snowy peaks. The land is brand new every time you start or join a game (everyone on the same server gets the same land), the minimap shows the part around you and names the biome you're in (the map key opens a much wider view, and pressing it again the big map, which can be dragged and zoomed within limits), the trees and bamboo grow like the real ones, and the zombies follow you out.

Underground & Caves

The ground goes 200 blocks down, under the city and the whole open world. Dig down through layers of rock - stone with pockets of granite, diorite, andesite, gravel and dirt, then a band of tuff, deepslate, a band of smooth basalt, deeper deepslate and blackstone - to bedrock at the very bottom. Ores sit at their own depths: coal and copper near the top, iron all the way down, lapis and gold deeper, redstone and diamonds near the bottom, and rare emeralds. Caves wind through the rock with bigger rooms further down, and out in the land there are cave entrances - sloping tunnels you can walk straight into that lead down to a cave room. Deep down there are underground cities: a huge domed cavern holding a lost city of deepslate streets, houses and towers with doorways and windows, glowstone street lamps, sculk on the empty lots and a plaza with a tall monument. Caves never cut under the city's streets or into villages, castles, pyramids, bridges or the underground cities' buildings, and the fog turns dark underground. Like the rest of the land, it's new every game.

Villages, Castles, Pyramids & Bridges

The open world has places people built. Villages have winding streets, a market with striped stalls around a well, lamp posts with paper lanterns, fields of crops, a hall with a bell tower, and houses you can walk into: timber-frame, plaster, plank or stone walls, thatch or shingle roofs that overhang the walls, shutters, flower bushes, a lantern over the door, a chimney, and a rug, bed and furniture inside. Each village is built from its own region's materials: oak, spruce, acacia or desert sandstone. Deserts also have pyramids: stepped sandstone with orange stripes and a gold top, climbable outside, with a passage into a tall chamber with pillars, sea lanterns and a gold altar. Castles stand on stone platforms. They have stone-brick walls with buttresses and arrow slits that you can walk along, a gate with banners, four corner towers and a tall keep (each with spiral steps up to its roof and a flag on top), a stable and a well. Wooden bridges with top rails and braces, and stone ones with arches, cross high over the rivers - room to walk or swim underneath - with gateposts and lamps at both ends. Roads and tracks blend into the land with frayed, grassy edges. The minimap shows them all, and the place you're in is named under it. They're different in every new world, but everyone on the same server gets the same ones.

Animals & Pets

Cows, pigs, sheep and chickens live out in the open world. They turn up in small groups on dry land around you (up to 12 at a time), wander about and stop to graze, and new ones keep appearing as you travel. Shoot or hit one and it runs away; kill it and it drops Meat that you pick up by walking over it - click with Meat held to heal. Look at one and press Use to tame it: it gets a red collar and follows you as your pet, catching up if you leave it behind, and your own shots never hurt it. Zombies leave animals alone. Pets stay with you until the game ends.

Map Editor Building Tools

Fill a whole area with one block, Replace one kind of block with another, and build walls, floors, hollow boxes, circles and balls with two clicks - a gold box shows the area first. Turn a copied build before pasting it, and one Undo takes back a whole fill, shape or paste.

Map Editor Time & Weather

Build by day, at sunset or at night under the stars (or let the day and night go round by themselves), with weather - light or hard rain with lightning and thunder, light or hard snow, a sandstorm, or Random (the default) to roll one every few minutes that fits the biome you're in - with rain and wind sounds, in Try Map and Play too. Rain and snow fall in the world itself and stop at roofs, and bad weather greys the sky, dims the light and brings the fog in. Games from the homepage always run the day-night cycle (a full day in ten minutes, starting in the morning) with Random weather, and the sky has a sun, a moon and drifting clouds.

Play Your Map: Zombie Waves

Press Play on the homepage (or Play in the Map Editor, on any map you build) to survive waves of blocky zombies. They find their way to you round walls, up stairs and ladders, and bash closed doors open, and each has a brain - some rush you, some curve round to come at you from the side, some sprint in bursts; every wave has more of them, a little faster and tougher. They come from all around you, wherever you are in the world, and each wave needs a number of kills - they keep coming until you reach it. Zombies you kill fall over away from the shot and fade away within a minute (fire burns bodies away in seconds). Windows crack on the first shot and shatter on the second, each in its own way. Your gun needs ammo, Loot Chests refill it and heal you once a wave, and your best wave on each map is kept.

Top Bar & Mob Size

In Map 1 and the Map Editor a bar runs along the top of the screen: GayZ, the game mode you're playing (or Map Editor / Try Map), your FPS, MS (how long each frame takes), your coordinates, then your device's chip and how many CPU cores it has, the weather and the time of day. Its colors follow the sky: darker and bluer at night, greyer in rain, paler in snow and sandier in a sandstorm. The wave, zombies left, kills and coins sit in a smaller line just under it. Turning off Settings > General > Show FPS Counter During Gameplay hides the FPS, MS and chip parts. Zombies, skeletons, your own body in the third-person views, other players and the camp's traders are all 2 blocks tall.

Breaking Blocks

While playing, hold the fire button on any block close to you and it cracks, then breaks. Each block takes its own time: leaves, glass and flowers go almost at once, dirt, sand and wool quickly, wood in about a second, stone and bricks longer, ores longer still, and obsidian and bedrock take a long time. Doors, slabs and stairs break like what they're made of. The game's own blocks (chests, spawners, the Player Start) can't be broken, and everything you break is back next game.

Play Offline

No internet? No problem. After you've opened GayZ online once, the whole game is kept on your device and plays with no connection: Play (Zombie Survival and every Game Mode), the Map Editor and your maps, Design a Skin, Inventory, the Store, Upgrades, Quests, Achievements, Settings and everything else single player. Things that need other players or the cloud - servers, chat, Friends, Clans, the Market, the Gallery, signing in and Cloud Save - are dimmed while you're offline and tell you they need internet, and the game says when you go offline and when you're back - also on Wi-Fi that has no internet, which the game notices by checking for itself. When you're online again, new updates arrive by themselves.

Real-World Map

In the World Map mode, pressing M twice opens a round 3D globe of the real Earth with you on it as an arrow: natural-coloured land, country borders and names, real cities and latitude/longitude lines, with the country you're in named at the bottom. Drag to spin it, scroll to zoom from the whole planet down to a few countries; zoomed in close it turns into the block map of the land around you.

World Map Mode: Pick Where on Earth

In the World Map game mode the land follows the real Earth - its oceans, coastlines and biomes where they really are, so walking east from Paris heads into Germany - and before the game a full-screen real-world map opens, with natural-looking land that fades from desert to forest to snow. Click a country and it zooms in to its states, provinces or regions (Canada's provinces and territories, the US states...); click one and its cities appear on the map and in a list - pick a city and Spawn Here starts you there (a country without regions lets you click any spot in it), Back goes up a level, and Spawn Randomly picks a random spot on land. The other game modes start in the city camp as before. Starting in a city builds a block town (with an uneven edge and little parks) in that city's style around you - glass skyscrapers in New York, Chicago, Dubai or Singapore, brick and plaster terraces in London, Paris or Berlin, towers and pagoda roofs in Tokyo or Seoul, sandstone and domes in Cairo or Istanbul, painted houses in Rio or Mexico City - with its landmark in the middle. Elsewhere, starting in a country with a famous landmark builds it out of blocks just ahead of you - the Eiffel Tower, Statue of Liberty, Big Ben, Colosseum, Great Wall, Taj Mahal, a pagoda, Christ the Redeemer, Sydney Opera House, St Basil's, the Parthenon, Chichén Itzá, the Pyramids of Giza, the Brandenburg Gate, a Dutch windmill, the CN Tower and the Burj Khalifa. East on the map is east in the game.

Inventory

Press Tab in a game (or the bag button on a phone) for your inventory: five armor slots (helmet, chestplate, leggings, boots, shield), a ten-by-ten grid and your hotbar. Your gun is in it (just the one you picked - Switch Weapon swaps it), blocks you break pop out as little spinning blocks that you pick up by walking near them, and Loot Chests drop health packs, ammo boxes, armor and blocks. Hold a block and right-click to place it (the fire button on a phone); click with a health pack or ammo box to use it. Worn armor blocks part of every hit. Drag items between slots (just the one picture moves), or Shift-click between the hotbar and the grid. In the Map Editor, Tab shows the blocks on the left and your hotbar plus a block storage on the right.

No Right-Click Menu

Right-clicking anywhere on GayZ - the game, Design a Skin and the info pages - doesn't open the browser's menu, so it never pops up over the game. Text boxes keep it for copy and paste.

Pause Menu & Saved Games

Press Escape while playing to pause: Back to Menu, Settings, Switch Weapon, Spectate, Save Progress and Leave Game. Spectate flies a free camera around (on a server it can also look through other players' eyes - click or press Use to switch), with nothing over the view, and Escape comes back to where you were. Switch Weapon opens the weapon picker in the middle of a game, and each gun keeps the ammo it had left. Spectate lets you fly around as yourself while the game keeps going - the land loads where you fly, nothing can hurt you, zombies can't see the camera (they go for the spot you flew off from), the very bottom of the world is a bedrock floor you can't fly under, and everything else works like playing: reload, Use, inspect, the map key, aiming, shooting and breaking blocks. Save Progress keeps your game - the wave, kills, coins, health, upgrades, gun and ammo, where you stand and the land around the city - and the next time you press Play you carry on from that wave. Back to Menu saves it the same way before going to the homepage; Leave Game ends the game for good. On a server there's nothing to save, so both just leave. The Map Editor keeps its own pause screen for making maps.

Choose Your Weapon

Every Map 1 run starts by picking the gun you take in - the Knife, Tomahawk, AK-47, M1911, Shotgun, AWP, GPMG, Flamethrower, Rocket Launcher or Void Ripper (more are Coming Soon) - each a clean, low-poly 3D model in the same dark, worn and scratched gunmetal (the Flamethrower keeps its red tank), with its own damage, fire rate, magazine and reload, shown as bars on its card. Automatic guns keep firing while you hold the button, holding right-click aims down the sights (zoomed in, steadier, tighter spreads) and letting go stops, every shot kicks the gun back smoothly with a muzzle flash and a puff of smoke, holding the zoom key looks far ahead, the Inspect Weapon key turns the gun so you can look at it, the Shotgun fires a spread, the Knife never runs out, the Knife and Tomahawk can be thrown while aiming (they fly spinning, hit harder than a swing and come back to your hand a second later), and the special guns have their own powers: the Flamethrower runs on fuel (a fuel tank bar, not bullets) and shoots a stream of flickering fire that sets zombies burning and wood and leaves alight - fire spreads along wood, races through trees and goes out in water or the rain, and burnt blocks fall away until the game ends, the Rocket and Grenade Launchers explode in a fireball that hurts everything near where they land, Crossbow bolts and AWP shots go through several zombies in a line, the Void Ripper slows zombies down with a purple burst, and the Tomahawk hits several zombies in one swing. Ammo from chests and the Trader comes in your gun's own amounts, and your last pick is remembered.

Map 1: The Block City & Safe Camp

Map 1 is a ready-made block city with winding roads, parks and buildings you can enter and climb. Seen from above it has an uneven outline, ringed with tall buildings. Playing it starts you in its walled camp, a safe zone with one main gate that zombies and skeletons can't get into. Four NPCs made in Design a Skin live there: the Trader sells Med Kits, Armor and Ammo Boxes, the Upgrader makes your gun hit harder, reload faster and hold more, Ammo Refill fills you up for free every so often, and the Quest Board gives quests for coins. Talk to one to open its board; close it with the red X or a click outside it. You earn coins from kills and cleared waves, and standing in the camp slowly heals you. The NPCs also stand in the camp while you build on Map 1 in the Map Editor.

Map 1 Game Modes, Difficulty & Mutators

Before you press Play, pick in Game Mode: a difficulty (Easy, Normal, Hard - Nightmare and Apex unlock later), a game mode - Classic (waves forever), Zombie Defense (hold out for 10 waves to win), Boss Hunt (a big, tough boss every 3 waves), Zombie Rush (barely a breather between waves, a survival timer, and no buying) or Zombie Extraction (after 5 waves a helicopter lands somewhere in the city - follow its red beam and hold its landing ring for 10 seconds while the zombies keep coming) - and mutators: Horde Rush, Loot Rush, Health Regen, Glass House, Iron Mode, Escalation and Boss Rush. Guest Mode plays a run without saving anything from it.

Skeletons

From wave 3 of Zombie Survival, real animated 3D skeletons join the zombies: they climb up out of the ground, walk, run and punch, flinch when hit and fall apart when killed before sinking away. Four kinds - quick Minions and Rogues, tough slow Warriors, and Mages in between - each with its own health, speed and hit. Everyone on a server sees the same skeletons.

Clean, Crisp Blocks & Guns

Every block in Map 1 and the Map Editor is simple, clean pixel art: a few flat colours per block, no speckles, drawn crisp and sharp however close you get instead of blurry. The guns have the same clean, flat look - low-poly and clear, never blurry - and both are light on your frame rate.

Map Editor Game Blocks

Player Start (where Play puts you), Zombie Spawner (where zombies come from), Loot Chest, Lever (opens the doors near it), Pressure Plate (opens them while someone stands on it) and the Glowing Sign you write your own words on.

Share Codes & Community Maps

Share Map puts your map online under a short code. Anyone can type a code - or pick a map from Community Maps, sorted by Newest, Most Liked or Most Played - and Play it against zombie waves or open it to Edit, in its own Shared Map slot so none of your maps are touched. Like the ones you enjoy (once each), report anything bad, and delete your own. Post to Gallery on the pause screen shares the map with a picture of it in the Gallery's Map section.

Gallery

The Gallery (beside Design a Skin at the bottom of the homepage) shows everyone's skins and maps in Character and Map sections, newest or most liked first. Post a skin from Design a Skin or a map from the Map Editor's pause screen, then anyone can open it, like it, comment on it, wear the skin or Play and Edit the map. Your own posts and the comments on them can be deleted.

Build Together

Start a room from your map and send friends its code - everyone in it builds on the same map live, sees each other as blocky figures with name tags, and every block, fill, paste, undo, door, lever and sign shows up for all of them.

Map Editor Slot Pictures

Every save slot shows a little picture of its map on the pause screen, so you can find the one you want.

Customization

Old & Golden Themes

Two complete looks for the menus, picked in Inventory > Theme: Old (the default - rusty metal, torn paper panels, worn profile card) and Golden (gold plates and frames). Both have rain falling over the homepage street, with splashes on the cobbles and the wet road shimmering, and the homepage rearranges itself to fit phones and tablets.

Design a Skin

A built-in skin editor (Design a Skin, at the bottom of the homepage): paint a blocky skin on a live 3D preview - at the classic size or with two, four, eight or sixteen times the pixels for finer detail - save it to slots, and wear it in game - or describe one in your own words ("cowgirl with red hair", "girl with a blonde ponytail") and it draws exactly that - not a preset style - understanding everyday words, plurals and typos, and "no" or "without" ("pirate without a hat"), with long hair falling all the way down the back. Randomize says what it made, and changing the pixel size redraws the skin with that much more detail (catchlights in the eyes, hair strands, woven cloth). Accessibility & Settings

Settings Help & Resets

Every setting has a "?" that explains it in a box styled like your theme. Every reset button is the same size and style, and each one asks "Are you sure?" first in a box that matches your theme. Text in Settings can't be selected - every button and box still works.

Auto Quality & FPS Cap

Auto Quality (on by default) first draws a little less far away when the game slows down, and only then makes the picture less sharp - sharpness comes back first when it can; an FPS Cap setting limits the frame rate. Both are in Settings > Graphics. Behind the scenes the game is built to stay smooth on slower devices: weapon pictures come ready-made, blocks are found quickly, new land is built with little work, zombies and skeletons are prepared before they appear, and the homepage's animations rest while you play.

View Distance

Settings > Graphics > View Distance (Auto, Short, Medium, Far, Max) sets how far away the city is drawn - fog fades out everything past it, so a shorter one runs much smoother on phones and older computers. Auto starts shorter on a phone, and if the game is still slow at its lowest resolution it draws less on its own.

Lite Textures & Graphics Recovery

Lite Textures (Settings > Graphics) draws the game's big pictures (like the gun models) at half size, so they use far less graphics memory. If your browser drops the game's graphics connection (often from several 3D games open at once), the game pauses with a small note instead of a big error, picks up right where you were when it comes back, and turns on Lite Textures so it's less likely to happen again.
