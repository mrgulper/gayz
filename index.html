Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 23, 2026

What GayZ is

GayZ is a free browser-based zombie survival game. This page explains what data the game collects and why, in plain language.

If you don't sign in

By default, GayZ doesn't need an account. Your progress, settings, and stats are saved only in your own browser's local storage. Nothing is sent anywhere, and nobody but you can see it.

If you sign in (Cloud Save)

  • You sign in with an account you already have (Google, Steam, GitHub or Discord), through Firebase Authentication - GayZ never sees or stores your password for that site.
  • The basic account info that site shares (your name and profile photo, plus your email if it shares one) is used only to identify your save data, not for ads or marketing.
  • Your save data (progress, coins, achievements, settings) is stored in Firebase (a Google-hosted database) so it can follow you across devices.

Nicknames, profiles, and leaderboards

Your nickname, Player ID, bio, and stats shown on your public profile (Profile panel > Shown to Public) are visible to other players wherever public features (leaderboards, chat, friend lookups) show them. Please don't put personal information (real name, contact details, etc.) in any field, since a field that's private today could become visible in a future update.

If you're signed in, starting a Team Battle saves your battle team as a "ghost team" that other players can see and battle: your nickname, which zombies and animals are on it, their rarity, how strong the team is and how many battles you've won. It's replaced each time you start another battle.

If you're signed in and play Speedrun mode, your best time goes on the leaderboard with your nickname, so other players can see the fastest runs. The ghost of your best run (where you went) stays on your device.

Chat, clans, friends and the Market

  • Chat messages you send (Global, server and clan chat) are stored online with your nickname so other players can read them.
  • Clans store their name, members and roles, and the XP members earn this month.
  • Friend requests, your friend list and your online status (online, in a game, or when you were last seen) are stored so your friends can see them.
  • Market listings store the skin, price and seller's nickname, and who bought it once it sells.

Gallery, shared maps and servers

  • Gallery posts (skins, maps and photos you take in Photo Mode) store the picture you post, its title, your nickname, likes, dislikes and comments - anyone can see them. Each like or dislike is saved under your account so you can only vote once per post.
  • Maps you share get a code and are stored online so anyone can play them; Build Together rooms store the edits and each player's spot while you build.
  • When you play on a server, your position, skin and nickname are sent to the other players in it while you play, along with what you hold, the hat and cape you wear, the emote you play, whether you're sitting in a vehicle, the boats, helicopters and hot-air balloons you put down, the map pins you share (where they are and whether they're for everyone or friends only; pins for friends only are sent only to the players in the server who are on your friends list), the spray tags you spray (your drawing and where it is), the blocks you place and break, and what you say in Global chat (shown over your head). A game you start from the homepage is a private server from the start, so friends can join it by its link; nobody sees it in the server list, and it's deleted when you leave.

Stored only on your device

Your settings, keybinds, theme colour, any homepage picture you upload and the list of settings you've changed (Settings > General > Your Settings Changes) stay in your own browser and aren't sent anywhere unless you sign in to Cloud Save (which copies your settings - including which inventory items you've locked - not the picture or the changes list).

Anonymous usage counters

A few features track simple aggregate counts (like how often a setting gets opened) to understand what's actually used. These are just totals - they aren't tied to your account or identity in any readable way.

What GayZ does not do

  • No ads, no ad tracking, no selling data to anyone.
  • No real-money payments - all in-game currency (coins/points) is just for the game, not real money.
  • No tracking you across other websites.

Your choices

You can play entirely without signing in. You can delete your own Gallery posts, comments and shared maps, take back Market listings, and leave or delete your clan at any time. Progression & Meta

19 Achievements

Spanning combat, survival and story, each with a hint instead of a bare "???" (skeleton hunters get Rattled, Bone Collector and Skeleton Key; the toughest runs earn Half Century, Slaughterhouse and Unstoppable; buying upgrades earns Well Equipped and Maxed Out; the deepest runs reach Sixty Waves and Never Ending, every difficulty, game mode and challenge mutator has its own, and the biggest single-game numbers earn Body Count, Killing Spree, Eagle Eye, Knife Party and Jackpot) — earned ones are bright, shiny gold cards, and locked ones stay easy to read - plus search, category filter and sort on one line, and a Print list that puts every achievement on one landscape page, eight across and nine down. On a phone, the Category and Sort boxes fit side by side without the panel scrolling sideways.

XP & Levels

Every game you finish earns XP - more for kills, waves survived, time alive and winning, and more on harder difficulties - shown on the game-over card, where an XP bar fills up from where your level was to where it is now; when it passes a level a LEVEL UP card pops up with a short fanfare, your new level in a gold coin and the coins it gave you (50 coins times the new level's number). Your level number on the profile card and next to your name changes straight away. XP is never spent (it isn't a currency) and raises your level with no top level, each one taking a little more XP than the last. Your level shows next to your name, and the level badge on your profile card opens the Levels page with your XP bar. Players from before XP started with XP from their lifetime kills and games. The Profile panel's Rank Roadmap still shows every rank and the lifetime kills it takes.

Career Stats & Profile

Lifetime totals, a stats-over-time chart, a pinnable highlight reel, a Best Run breakdown, an anniversary line, today's session stats, and a "Nearly There" panel toward trackable achievements - plus a Bio others can see, private Notes only you can see, and a Trophies tab (see Trophies & Trophy Room).

Trophies & Trophy Room

15 trophies for your best records - zombies killed, best wave, kill streak, skeletons, games played, hours played, coins earned, your level, assists, achievements, creatures caught, your rarest catch, Team battles won, the Zombie Book and your best speedrun time. Each comes in bronze, silver and gold. The Trophies tab on your Profile shows them all, with what the next one up needs. On Map 1 the Safe Camp has a Trophy Room against one of its walls: a booth with your cups on two shelves (dark grey while still locked) - look at a cup to see its name and tier. On a server, everyone sees their own trophies there.

Goals

Pick up to 3 personal goals to track/checklist on your Profile.

Upgrades & Points

Every Map 1 run earns Points (the blue star next to your coins) from its kills and waves. Spend them in Upgrades' Zombie Survival tab on boosts that last for every run after: more health and armor, more gun damage and headshot damage, faster reloads, more speed, starting coins, more coins from kills, more spare ammo, softer zombie hits, slow healing (Second Wind), a cheaper Shop and faster healing in the camp (each card grows a little when you point at it), with third picks after many of them (Kevlar Vest, Berserker, Gold Rush, Haggler and more) - 26 in all. Once you've survived to wave ten, Prestige trades your Points and Upgrades for 10% more Points from every run, per level - and each level costs more: 100,000 Points for the first, 200,000 for the second, and so on.

Quests

Rolling quests that come and go (kill zombies, reach a wave, finish runs), counted from your Map 1 runs and claimed for coins in the Quests panel. Each quest is a big card, two to a row, filled with its rarity's colour like the Store's crates - Common to Legendary, the Store's colours, rarer for harder quests - with corner rivets, dark writing, a dark rarity label and a progress bar; the cards fade in one after another and the list fades out at the bottom. A search box and an All rarities menu at the top find the ones you want. Claiming a quest makes just that card fade away - the rest of the list stays where it is. The Quests button shows how many are finished and waiting to be claimed in a little circle, and pointing at it changes nothing - Upgrades, Friendship and Achievements show their counts the same way.

Daily Challenge

One goal a day, the same for everyone, on a wide gold card at the top of the Quests: take lots of zombies down, in one run or on Hard and up, take them down with a melee weapon, take down skeletons, Rare-or-rarer zombies or bosses, reach a high wave (some days on Hard and up), or get a long kill streak - 10 kinds, each easy, medium or hard. They're tough on purpose (the easiest kill goal is 120 zombies), and harder pays more coins and XP. The game tells you the moment it's done, and you claim it in the Quests. Claim it on days in a row to build a streak: every day of streak adds 25% to the reward, up to 2.5 times on day 7 and after. Miss a day and the streak starts again. A new challenge comes at midnight your time - it shows up by itself even with the page left open - with a countdown on the card. It's on the homepage too, just under Play: today's goal, how far along you are and your streak, and "Claim!" once it's done - click it to open the Quests.

Homepage Extras

Small cards on the homepage: your last run and best wave, a tip of the day (12 of them, a new one each day), the top 3 players by best wave, and an Invite a friend button that copies the game's link (or opens your phone's share menu). Pick how they show in Settings > General > Homepage: Cards puts them in the bottom corners (as many as fit your screen), Today Panel keeps the homepage clean and puts them all behind one Today button. Play gets a Continue tag when you left a run halfway (it picks it up on the same mode and difficulty), and a New here? tag that opens How to Play before your first run. Tap your character for a dance, wave or flex; now and then a zombie peeks in from the side, and snow falls in December. Your login streak is on your Profile, and the Daily Challenge lives in the Quests.

World & Exploration

Build Mode / Map Editor

A standalone creative sandbox. The Map Editor button first asks Worlds (nature biomes all around you, everywhere) or Plain (your own slots, on flat grass that goes on forever in every direction - no edge, no void). Hundreds of pixel-art blocks (every one with a slab and stairs), a Tab panel split exactly in half - the blocks to pick on the left, your own 10 by 10 block inventory with big slots on the right (drag blocks into it; click one to put it in your hotbar at the bottom of the screen), water and lava that flow (where a shore turns a corner the water's edge curves round with a line of foam and shallow, sandy water filling the corner, instead of making a square corner; swim in water, jump off the bottom and climb out onto a ledge), free-fly flight, save slots plus the ready-made Map 1 city, Try Map to walk it in first person with a gun (or third person from behind or in front - the camera key switches), Export/Import Map, Mirror mode (N - a see-through wall shows where it copies to), a Line tool, and Copy/Paste for stamping down builds.

Voting Block: stand on it to vote - the number of players on Voting Blocks shows above it. Gravity Flip: near it you fall upward. Shrink Pad: shrink small enough to fit through gaps one block high. Grow Pad: grow big - you see from up high and hit harder. Mirror Block: zombies that bite you near it take some of the bite back. Ghost Block: you walk straight through it, zombies can't. Weather Stone: change the weather. Day/Night Switch: switch between day and night (both go back to normal when you stop). Random Block: turns into a random block the moment you place it. Invisibility Pad: zombies lose track of you for a few seconds. Each block has its own picture with a symbol on it. Nothing about them is saved beyond the blocks themselves - every try or game starts them fresh.

Shop

Press B in any Zombie Survival game (Settings > Controls > Shop), or the cart button on a phone, to open the Shop. Its 5 tabs: Items (Med Kits, Armor, Ammo Boxes and a Fishing Rod), Traps (packs of Spike Blocks, Bear Traps, Mines, Explosive Barrels and Bait Blocks), Upgrades (more gun damage, faster reloads and a bigger magazine, three levels each), Ammo (a free refill of your gun every 30 seconds) and Quests (up to three at a time - kill zombies, open chests, land headshots, reach a wave - claimed for coins). You pay with the coins you earn from kills and cleared waves in that game. Close it with B, Escape, the red X or a click outside it. On Map 1 the camp's four people still stand there, and talking to one opens the Shop on their tab.

Traps

In Zombie Survival you buy traps with coins in the Shop's Traps tab (press B) and place them like any block - Spike Blocks that hurt zombies walking over them, a Bait Block that pulls zombies toward it, Mines, Bear Traps that hold a zombie in place, or Explosive Barrels. Loot chests sometimes have one too, and so do some rewards (like rescuing a survivor). Shoot an Explosive Barrel to blow it up: every zombie close by takes a big hit, barrels next to it go off as well, and it hurts you too if you're standing too close. Lure a wave over a line of barrels with bait, then fire. Explosive Barrels are in the Map Editor's Special category too.

World Events

From the second wave of Zombie Survival, something unexpected happens every few minutes. A supply plane flies over and drops a crate on a parachute nearby - follow its light beam and walk up to it for ammo, health packs, a trap and coins before it's gone. A zombie horde migrates across the map past you - they leave you alone unless you get close or shoot one. Or a glowing meteor streaks down and blows up nearby: the zombies around it mutate into bigger, purple, sparking mutants that take more shots, run faster, hit harder and pay extra coins, and a few more climb out of the crater.

Golden Runner: a glowing gold zombie turns up under a gold light beam and runs away from you. Every few seconds it stops to dance and taunt you, and it gets tired as it goes - sprint after it and touch it to catch it, and at least 100 coins burst out (more on later waves). Leave it for 50 seconds and it digs into the ground and escapes.

Blood Moon: from wave 2, about one night in 3 the moon rises red. The sky, the light and the moon turn blood red, a red glow pulses round the edge of the screen, and until sunrise every wave has double the zombies, twice as many at once, coming twice as fast - and every coin you earn counts double. On a server everyone gets the same Blood Moon.

Survivors to Rescue

One of Zombie Survival's world events: someone gets stuck on a rooftop nearby, waving and shouting for help, with a green light beam over them. Climb up and reach them before they give up to rescue them. Every other survivor joins you - they follow you around and shoot the zombies near you (zombies leave them alone) - and the rest give you a quick quest instead: kill a set number of zombies in time for coins and a trap. Everyone you rescue then moves into your camp for good (up to 8): in your later games on Map 1 they stand guard in the camp and shoot zombies that come near its walls. Look at one and press Use to give an order - Follow Me (up to 3 at once) or Hold Here, where they stay put and keep shooting. Survivors only live in your own games, not on servers.

Fishing

Buy a Fishing Rod in the Shop (press B) for 60 coins (loot chests sometimes have one too). Hold it, look at water and click to cast: the bobber flies out and floats. When something bites, the bobber dips and "Click now!" shows - click quickly to reel it in, or it gets away. You might pull out a Fish or a rare Golden Fish (eat them to heal), an Old Boot, a sunken treasure chest full of coins and an item, or a Zombie Fish that leaps out of the water at you. Out of 5 kinds of catch, fish are the most common.

Zombie Radios

Old radios turn up on the ground as you explore, with a yellow music note bobbing over them. Walk up to one and it crackles into one of 10 silly news reports - and often a real tip too: a supply plane on its way to you. Each radio plays once.

Spray Tags

Draw your own 16 x 16 spray tag in the pause screen's Spray Paint, with 15 paints and an eraser. In a game, look at a block close by and press Y (Settings > Controls) - or the spray-can button on a phone - to spray it on. Your last 3 stay up; breaking the block takes one off. On a server everyone sees them; turn off Settings > Controls > Show Other Players' Sprays to hide others' sprays, and a player you mute never shows theirs.

Crafting

Open your inventory in a game: Crafting is on the left and your bag on the right, side by side (on a narrow phone screen, Bag and Crafting tabs switch between them). Every recipe shows what it makes, what it needs (how many you have and how many it takes) and a Craft button. There are 15 recipes: gear - Goo Disguise, Boat, Helicopter, Heli Fuel, Health Pack, Ammo Box, Catch Traps, all five armor pieces, the four Potions, the Jetpack and Jetpack Fuel, the Metal Detector and (in winter) Snowballs - and blocks - Crop Plot, Oak Planks, Crate, Stone Bricks, Glass and Bricks. Most take blocks you break (any log, any stone, any dirt or grass, any sand, any planks), so you don't need one exact kind. Two new materials: Zombie Goo, which about 45% of the zombies you kill drop, and Plant Fiber, which comes with every Crop Plot harvest. If your bag is full, what you made lands at your feet.

Goo Disguise

Craft it from Zombie Goo, hold it and click to smear it on. For 30 seconds zombies don't notice you and walk right past - a slimy green edge round the screen shows how long is left. Shooting or swinging at anything washes it off at once. On someone else's server their zombies can still see you.

Camp Farm

Place a Crop Plot (craft one from dirt and a log) inside your camp's walls on Map 1 and it joins your camp farm: unlike other blocks you place in a game, it stays there for every later game. Press Use on it to plant seeds; a farm crop takes 5 minutes of real time to grow, and keeps growing while you're away or the game is closed (Sprinklers and Greenhouse Glass still speed it up). Harvest it for Vegetables (eat them to heal) and Plant Fiber for crafting, then plant again. Break a plot to take it off the farm. The farm only exists in your own games, not on servers.

Boats & Helicopters

Craft a Boat (planks and logs) or a Helicopter (stone, glass, planks and Zombie Goo), hold it and click: a boat goes on the water in front of you, a helicopter on open ground in front of you. Right-click one, or press Use beside it, to get in - you sit in the seat (watch yourself with P, and others on a server see you sitting) - and right-click or Use again to get out (Crouch gets you out of a boat too). A boat only moves on water - forward and back, left and right to steer. A helicopter flies the way you look: forward, back and sideways to move, Jump to climb and Crouch to go down, up to about 40 blocks over the ground, and you can still shoot from it. It burns fuel while it's up: a full tank is 60 seconds of flying (a new helicopter comes full). Craft Heli Fuel from Zombie Goo and a log and use it in or next to one - or just carry some, and an empty tank fills itself from it. Out of fuel, it sinks slowly to the ground. To get out of a helicopter, land first. You can have up to 4 out at once, and on a server everyone sees your boats and helicopters.

Hot-Air Balloon

Craft a Hot-Air Balloon (Plant Fiber, planks and Zombie Goo), hold it and click to put it down on open ground with room above it, then right-click it or press Use to climb into the basket. You only steer up and down: hold Jump to light the burner and rise, Crouch to let air out and sink faster, or do neither and it slowly drifts down. The wind carries you along - the higher you are, the faster it blows - up to about 60 blocks over the ground. The burner runs on Heli Fuel: a full tank is 60 seconds of burning (a new balloon comes full), and a can in your bag fills it again on its own. Out of fuel, it floats slowly down. Land to get out. It counts toward your vehicles out at once, and on a server everyone sees it.

Go-Kart

Every Zombie Survival run parks a rickety blocky go-kart a few steps from where you start. Walk up to it and right-click it, or press Use (the use button on a phone), to hop in and sit down in the seat (press P to see yourself; on a server others see you sitting), and right-click, Use or Crouch to hop out beside it. Forward and back drive and reverse, left and right steer (on a phone, the move stick does both), and you can still look around and shoot while you drive. It drives up one-block steps - up hills and mountains too, gliding up each step instead of jumping - drops off ledges and bounces off walls. Ram a zombie at speed to run it over - at full speed that's about a regular zombie's whole health - but watch out: zombies can still bite you in the seat.

Heaven & Heaven's Peaks

High above the land, from 300 to 500 blocks up, is Heaven: a floor of soft white clouds you can walk on (with a few holes to fall through), floating islands - cloud islands, grassy gardens with glowstone, gold islands and little quartz temples with a glowing roof and a gold altar - and a cloud staircase winding up from the cloud floor to every island. The air turns a bright warm white up there, and angels live in it. There are no portals: build a tower all the way up, or climb a Heaven's Peak - now and then a giant lone mountain rises out of the land, snowy and icy near the top, climbing about a block per step so you can jump your way up, and its summit comes out through the clouds. Like the rest of the land, Heaven is new every game; it only appears once you're high up.

Open World

Map 1's city isn't the edge of the world any more: its roads run out through open gates as dirt tracks into an open world that goes on forever, with no edge in any direction. The land grows around you as you go, with 26 biomes from all over the Earth - oceans (deep, frozen, and warm ones with thin, branchy coral plants and seagrass you can swim through), beaches, rivers, plains, oak, birch, dark and cherry forests, taiga, snowy tundra and ice spikes, deserts with cacti, savanna, badlands mesas, jungles and bamboo, swamps and mangroves, mushroom fields, and mountains with snowy peaks. The land is brand new every time you start or join a game (everyone on the same server gets the same land), the minimap shows the part around you and names the biome you're in, drawn like a real map - sharp, with about one dot of colour for every block and pixel on your screen, smooth colours instead of single blocks, hills lit from one side so you can see the land's shape, and clean lines along shores and roads - (the map key opens a much wider view, pressing it again the same-size map in the middle of the screen, which can be dragged anywhere and zoomed in and out as far as you like without lagging, and once more back to the small one), the trees and bamboo grow like the real ones, and the zombies follow you out.

Underground & Caves

The ground goes 200 blocks down, under the city and the whole open world. Dig down through layers of rock - stone with pockets of granite, diorite, andesite, gravel and dirt, then a band of tuff, deepslate, a band of smooth basalt, deeper deepslate and blackstone - and then, 300 blocks down, the Demon Realm: the rock turns to netherrack, soul sand and blackstone, and huge caverns open up with red fungus floors, glowing magma and glowstone in the roof, dark red fog and no sky. Demons live down there. Bedrock is at the very bottom, and the only way down is to dig. Ores sit at their own depths: coal and copper near the top, iron all the way down, lapis and gold deeper, redstone and diamonds near the bottom, and rare emeralds. Caves wind through the rock with bigger rooms further down, and out in the land there are cave entrances - sloping tunnels you can walk straight into that lead down to a cave room and on through a second tunnel into a bigger cave - never a dead end. Deep down there are underground cities: a huge domed cavern holding a lost city of deepslate streets, houses and towers with doorways and windows, glowstone street lamps, sculk on the empty lots and a plaza with a tall monument. Caves never cut under the city's streets or into villages, castles, pyramids, bridges or the underground cities' buildings, and the fog turns dark underground. Like the rest of the land, it's new every game.

Villages, Castles, Pyramids & Bridges

The open world has places people built. Villages have winding streets, a market with striped stalls around a well, lamp posts with paper lanterns, fields of crops, a hall with a bell tower, and houses you can walk into: timber-frame, plaster, plank or stone walls, thatch or shingle roofs that overhang the walls, shutters, flower bushes, a lantern over the door, a chimney, and a rug, bed and furniture inside. Each village is built from its own region's materials: oak, spruce, acacia or desert sandstone. Deserts also have pyramids: stepped sandstone with orange stripes and a gold top, climbable outside, with a passage into a tall chamber with pillars, sea lanterns and a gold altar. Castles stand on stone platforms. They have stone-brick walls with buttresses and arrow slits that you can walk along, a gate with banners, four corner towers and a tall keep (each with spiral steps up to its roof and a thin cloth flag waving in the wind on a slim pole with a gold ball on top - each castle flies one design: the GayZ face, a striped banner or a zombie skull crest, in its own colours), a stable and a well. Wooden bridges with top rails and braces, and stone ones with arches, cross high over the rivers - room to walk or swim underneath - with gateposts and lamps at both ends. Roads and tracks blend into the land with frayed, grassy edges. The minimap shows them all, and the place you're in is named under it. They're different in every new world, but everyone on the same server gets the same ones.

134 kinds of animals, at their real sizes, live out in the open world, each only where it fits: farm animals, dogs, cats and ducks on the plains; deer, bears, foxes, squirrels, owls and bugs in the forests; moose, elk and beavers in the taiga; polar bears, reindeer, musk oxen and snowy owls in the snow; lions, elephants, rhinos, cheetahs, kangaroos and ostriches on the savanna; tigers, jaguars, gorillas, monkeys, sloths, tapirs and peacocks in the jungle; bison and boars on the plains, lynxes in the woods, yaks, llamas, alpacas and snow leopards up the mountains, gazelles and wildebeest on the savanna, capybaras by the water and red pandas in the bamboo; bulls, ponies, mules, hares, hamsters and guinea pigs on the plains; black bears, wolverines, ferrets, moles, bats, ravens, kiwis, snails, mantises and fireflies in the woods; huskies in the snow; fennec foxes, jackals, roadrunners, cobras, tortoises and vultures in the desert; cougars and bighorn sheep in the badlands; ibexes and marmots up the mountains; impalas, water buffalo, aardvarks, pangolins, dingoes, quokkas and Tasmanian devils on the savanna; okapis, mandrills, gibbons, toucans, macaws, pythons, sun bears and black panthers in the jungle; herons, axolotls and crayfish in swamps and rivers; puffins on the beach; orcas, hammerhead sharks and pufferfish at sea; big friendly spiders in the forests and jungle; camels, scorpions and snakes in the desert; alligators, frogs and dragonflies in swamps; seals, crabs and starfish on the beach; and whales, dolphins, sharks, octopuses, jellyfish and fish swimming in the sea and rivers. The newest: oxen, roosters, mammoths, sabre-tooth cats, mantis shrimps, sea cucumbers, sponges, conches, blobfish, stonefish, frigatebirds, leeches, cockroaches, termites, queen and soldier ants and dark ladybugs - and made-up creatures too, all Legendary: dragons, unicorns, pegasi, griffins, phoenixes, krakens, lake monsters, yetis, sasquatches, jackalopes, cerberuses, hydras and manticores. Some animals are dangerous: wolves, bears, polar bears, big cats, crocodiles, sharks, snakes and a few others chase you if you come too close and bite - shooting one makes it angrier. Every animal that attacks looks the part: a darker coat, angry brows, a scar, bared fangs and red eyes that glow in the dark. Bugs and small birds flutter a little above the ground. They're all built from chunky blocks with painted faces - eyes with a white, a coloured middle, a pupil and a shine, a brow, nostrils and a mouth - furry patterned coats and hooves or paws; on In Real Life their bodies are rounder and softer. In the Map Editor, pick Animals in the Tab panel's list, choose one and right-click to put it down - it walks about there, while you build and when you play - and left-click it to take it away; they're saved with the map. Wild ones turn up in small groups around you (up to 12 at a time), wander about and stop to graze, and new ones keep appearing as you travel. Shoot or hit one and it runs away; kill it and it drops its own raw meat - Raw Beef from a cow, Raw Pork from a pig, Raw Mutton from a sheep, Raw Chicken, Raw Venison from a deer, Raw Rabbit, a raw fish or bird by its name (Raw Salmon, Raw Duck), and Raw Lion Meat, Raw Bear Meat and so on from the rest - that you pick up by walking over it; click with it held to eat it and heal. Look at one and press Use to tame it: it gets a red collar and follows you as your pet, catching up if you leave it behind, and your own shots never hurt it. Very rarely - 1% of the animals that turn up in their biomes, and always alone - you meet one of 20 dinosaurs, built from the same chunky blocks at their real sizes, from a chicken-sized Compsognathus to a Brachiosaurus taller than a house: T. rex, Giganotosaurus, Spinosaurus, Allosaurus, Carnotaurus, Megalosaurus, Dilophosaurus, Velociraptor and Compsognathus eat meat - they chase the nearest player they see and bite hard, and shooting one makes it angrier instead of scaring it off - while Triceratops, Stegosaurus, Ankylosaurus, Brachiosaurus, Diplodocus, Apatosaurus, Brontosaurus, Parasaurolophus, Iguanodon and Pachycephalosaurus graze and run when hurt, and Pteranodons glide high over beaches and mountains. The big meat-eaters are huge and heavy - a T. rex stands about eight blocks tall - with thick thighs, a deep jaw, a muscly chest and twice the health. Dinosaurs can't be tamed. Zombies leave animals alone - but animals don't trust them: one that comes close sends chickens running in circles, makes cows stop and stare, and sends the rest bolting away. Pets stay with you until the game ends. On a server everyone sees the same animals, and a pet follows whoever tamed it.

Catching & Team Battles

Catch zombies and animals and build a battle team. Every game starts with 3 Catch Traps in the last hotbar slot, and Loot Chests often have one or two more. Hold a Catch Trap and click to throw it at a zombie or a wild animal - no need to weaken it first: it flies in an arc and snaps shut on the first one it touches. Most are caught straight away, but rarer ones and bosses break free more often, and if one breaks free the trap is used up. Every zombie and animal also shows a health bar above it for a few seconds after it's hurt. Caught ones leave the game and go to the Pets tab in Inventory, where you can keep up to 200. Each shows its rarity, health and attack, which come from what it was: a boss zombie or a Legendary dragon is far tougher than a Common cow. Click creatures to put up to 3 on your team, then press Battle!. Your team fights another player's ghost team - a team someone saved by battling - about as strong as yours, or a Wild Team when nobody else's can be found, in a fenced grass arena. The battle plays itself: every creature goes for the nearest enemy, and each has a special power from what it is - plain zombies get stronger together (Horde), skeletons throw bones from a few blocks away, monster zombies hit harder when hurt (Rage), bosses Stomp everyone near their target, hunting animals Pounce for a double first hit, dinosaurs Roar to weaken the other team, big animals have Thick Hide and the rest Heal Friends. The power shows on each creature's card. Watch it in 3D or 2D (from straight above) - switch with the button on the battle or in Settings > Controls > Team Battle View - and speed it up to two or four times as fast. The last team standing wins, or the one with more health left after 120 seconds. A win pays 30 coins and a loss 5, and the Pets tab counts your wins and losses. On a server everyone can catch: the trap shakes on the one it hit for a moment while the host's game decides.

Zombie Book

Inventory > Zombie Book has a card for every kind of zombie - 27 of them: the regular zombie, the boss, the skeletons, the monster zombies, and the ones that only live in Heaven, the Demon Realm or the ocean. Each card shows the zombie in 3D with a few words about it (fast, tough, hits hard, bursts, where it lives). Ones you haven't met yet are dark shadows named ???. A kind counts as met the first time one appears in your game (on a server too), which pays 10 coins, and meeting the last one pays 200 more and puts a Complete! badge on the page.

Shrinking Zone Mode

Picked in Game Modes. The zombie waves come as usual, but a glowing green wall rings a safe zone 100 blocks across from its middle. It waits, then closes in to a smaller circle - 6 circles in all, each one inside the last, down to just 7 blocks. The minimap shows the zone in green and the next one as a dashed white ring, and the top line says how long until it shrinks. Outside the wall is toxic fog: a green haze over your screen tells you how far the safe zone is, and zombies out there run 1.7 times as fast. Survive the last circle and you win - a whole game takes about 5 minutes. It works on servers too: everyone gets the same zone.

Infection Mode

A game of zombie tag, picked in Game Modes. There are no zombie waves: one player turns into a zombie, and every human an infected one touches turns too. Bots fill the game up to 6 players, so you can play it alone, and friends join with the invite link (the address bar, or Invite Friends in the pause menu) - each friend takes a bot's place at the next round. A round starts after 8 seconds, and the first zombie is held still for 3 seconds so the humans can run. Infected players look like zombies to everyone, run faster, have a green edge round the screen and can't shoot. Humans can't hurt the infected, but a shot stuns one for 2.5 seconds: it slows right down and can't tag anyone. If everyone is infected the zombies win; anyone still human after 150 seconds wins. Humans who survive get 25 coins and the infected get 15 when the zombies win, and the next round starts a few seconds later.

Pinata Zombies

About one zombie in 300 is a Pinata Zombie: a paper-party body glowing in sliding candy colours with a Pinata tag. Smash it and it bursts into confetti, and whoever got it wins a candy rain: a handful of Candy (eat it to heal a little) and 60 extra coins on top of the kill.

Snowball Fights

A winter event, from December to February: you start every game with 8 Snowballs and get 4 more each wave, loot chests have some, and a Snow block crafts into four. Hold one and click to throw it in an arc - the zombie it hits is frozen in a block of ice for 3 seconds and can't move or bite. Settings > Snowballs All Year turns them on all year. Snowballs from other players on a server freeze zombies too.

Metal Detector

Craft one (stone, glass and Zombie Goo) or find one in a loot chest. While you hold it, treasure is buried somewhere nearby: it beeps faster the closer you get, and a Signal bar fills up. Stand right on the spot and click to dig up coins and a random find, then a new treasure is hidden. Click anywhere else for a "cold / warm / very close" hint.

Riding Animals

Look at one of your own tamed animals that's big enough to sit on (horses, cows, camels, bears and more - not tiny ones, swimmers, flyers or dinosaurs) and press Use to climb on. Move to ride where you look, Jump to hop, and Use again to get off. You can still look around and shoot while riding. Alone or as a server's host.

Potions

Four potions, crafted or found in loot chests: Speed (run much faster), Invisibility (zombies lose track of you), Giant (much bigger, hitting harder) and Tiny (small enough for one-block gaps). Hold one and click to drink it - it lasts 30 seconds, with a timer above your hotbar. Giant and Tiny cancel each other.

Map Pins & Compass

Open the big map (press M twice, or tap the corner map on a phone) and click or tap anywhere to drop a pin there - up to 3, and dropping another takes the oldest away. A little menu pops up beside the pin to remove it; on a server it also lets you pick who sees it: Everyone, Friends (only the players there who are on your friends list get it) or Only me. Click a pin again to open its menu. While any pin is out a compass runs along the top of the screen with North, East, South and West and every pin - yours, and the ones other players share with you, marked with their first letter - and how many blocks away it is. A pin behind you sits at the edge, on the side to turn to.

Jetpack

Craft a Jetpack (or, rarely, find one in a loot chest). It works from your bag: jump, then keep Jump held in the air to fly up, with flames under your feet. A full tank lasts 8 seconds of flying, and when it runs dry a Jetpack Fuel from your bag fills it again on its own. A fuel bar shows while the tank isn't full.

Speedrun Mode

A game mode against the clock: clear 5 waves as fast as you can, with short breaks. The timer runs above your hotbar next to your best time (it turns red once you're behind). Beat your best and your run is saved - next time a see-through blue ghost of you runs it alongside you. Signed in, your best time goes on the leaderboard, and the end screen shows the world's 5 fastest.

Party Modes

Four game modes with no zombie waves, played in rounds on servers or alone with bots filling the game up to 6 players. Hide and Seek: the seeker is blindfolded for 20 seconds while everyone hides, and everyone found joins the seekers. Capture the Flag: Red against Blue - carry the other team's flag home, and shoot an enemy to stun them and make them drop it; first to 3. King of the Hill: stand alone on the glowing hill for a point a second, first to 45. Zombie Soccer: Red against Blue, run into the giant zombie head (or shoot it) to kick it into the other goal; first to 3. A coloured diamond over everyone's head shows their team. Winners get 25 coins.

Dungeons

Deep under the land around the city and in every Open World world lie dungeons: a grid of stone-brick rooms joined by corridors, lit by glowstone, about 36 blocks across. A sloped tunnel leads down from the land to the door (or dig or cave your way in). Some rooms hold loot chests. The big Boss Room in the middle has two more chests - and the first time you walk in, a boss zombie wakes up with guards.

Map Editor Building Tools

Fill a whole area with one block, Replace one kind of block with another, and build walls, floors, hollow boxes, circles and balls with two clicks - a gold box shows the area first. Turn a copied build before pasting it, and one Undo takes back a whole fill, shape or paste.

Map Editor Time & Weather

Build by day, at sunset or at night under the stars (or let the day and night go round by themselves), with weather - drizzle, very light, light, slightly hard or hard rain, thunderstorms, freezing rain, ice storms, very light to hard snow, heavy snowstorms, blizzards, sleet, hail, frost, cold snaps, fog, heatwaves, dust storms, a sandstorm, or Random (the default) to roll one every few minutes that fits the biome you're in, changing a step at a time - a thunderstorm eases to hard rain, slightly hard, light, very light, drizzle and then clear, each step blending smoothly into the next over about half a minute (the rain thins out, the sky slowly brightens, the sound fades), never all at once, and snow sometimes turns to sleet for a while as it ends - (snow and hail can even reach the city now and then) - with rain and wind sounds, in Try Map and Play too. Rain, snow and hail fall in the world itself and stop at roofs - rain as thin streaks of different lengths leaning with the wind that splash little rings on the ground, snow as real six-armed snowflakes with little side branches when they're close (soft flakes further away) of all sizes that slowly turn, flutter and drift, rain close by as clear teardrop-shaped water drops with a dark edge and a bright glint, and hail as small hard ice pellets that streak down and bounce off the ground - and bad weather darkens the sky, rolls low storm clouds in under the high ones and dims the light. The ground out in the open gets wet and dark with puddles that mirror the sky (wet patches and puddles end in soft rounded shapes, never square block corners), snow slowly piles up in white patches on top of blocks and hail leaves little round ice stones about; under a roof stays dry, and it all dries or melts away a while after the weather passes, but only fog, a sandstorm and hard snow close the view in - rain leaves it clear, and on a clear day far-away land and buildings stay sharp right to the edge of the view. When rain or hail clears up in the daytime there's a one in 10 chance of a rainbow for a couple of minutes, opposite the sun like a real one - a bright bow from red outside to violet inside, a fainter second bow above it with its colours flipped, and the sky a little lighter inside; more rain or nightfall fades it away. Very, very rarely, a solar eclipse turns the day dark: the moon covers the sun, a white glow rings it and the stars come out. Games from the homepage always run the day-night cycle (a full day in ten minutes, starting in the morning) with Random weather, and the sky looks real - deeper blue overhead fading lighter to the horizon, a sun with a white-hot middle, a patchy surface and an uneven glow, a cratered moon that goes through its phases - crescent, half, full and back - a new one each game day, soft fluffy clouds that drift and change shape (in rain and snow they become dark storm clouds with lighter edges, still in clear shapes with gaps between them, and they stay over the night sky too), and at night thousands of tiny twinkling stars, a few bright, some bluish or warm - only once it's really dark. Holding the zoom key brings out fainter stars, like binoculars.

Wind, Lightning & Natural Disasters

Every weather has its own wind - barely a breath on a clear day, a gale in a blizzard - that slowly turns, blows the rain, snow and dust along with it, and gently pushes you when you're out in the open (never under a roof). Lightning forks down from the clouds to the ground all round you with a bright white core, a violet glow and branches, flickering like real strokes and lighting up the sky, with the thunder arriving a moment later - later and deeper the farther away it struck; softer flashes glow inside the clouds with only a low rumble. In the worst cold, frost creeps in from the edges of your screen; in a heatwave the air shimmers and glows warm. Freezing rain glazes the ground so you slide about. And now and then, rarely and only where it could really happen, one of 20 natural disasters strikes: floods and flash floods (in very hard rain or by lots of water), droughts, wildfires and forest fires that spread with the wind, avalanches on snowy slopes, gas pouring out of a lake, earthquakes that shake the ground and crack it open, tsunamis by the sea, volcanic eruptions with ash columns and glowing lava bombs, landslides, mudslides and rockfalls, sinkholes, sinking ground, solar flares, geomagnetic storms with the northern lights, and - as rarely as an eclipse - meteoroids, asteroids and comets crashing down. They hurt you if you're caught and change the natural ground a little (craters, cracks, burnt grass, fallen rocks); turn on Settings > General > Disasters Can Break Anything and they can wreck your builds too, though your camp is always safe. An impact gives you 10x luck for 24 game hours, so rarer animals and zombies turn up far more often. In the Map Editor the Disaster button starts any of them to watch, and on a server everyone sees the same one.

Play Your Map: Zombie Waves

Press Play on the homepage (or Play in the Map Editor, on any map you build) to survive waves of blocky zombies. Walking speeds up and slows down smoothly, and your view glides up a step instead of snapping. They find their way to you round walls, up stairs and ladders, and bash closed doors open, and each has a brain - some rush you, some curve round to come at you from the side, some sprint in bursts; every wave has more of them, a little faster and tougher. They come from all around you, wherever you are in the world - never closer than 30 blocks, so you see them coming - and each wave needs a number of kills - they keep coming until you reach it. On a clear, sunny day, zombies standing under open sky catch fire and slowly burn - a roof, shade or water saves them, and one that burns up before anyone hits it isn't anybody's kill; in rain, snow, fog, a storm or at night they're as dangerous as ever. Zombies down in the caves are tougher and bite harder but turn up less often, and the weather up top never reaches them. Zombies you kill fall over away from the shot and fade away within a minute (fire burns bodies away in seconds). Windows crack on the first shot and shatter on the second, each in its own way. Your gun needs ammo, Loot Chests refill it and heal you once a wave, and your best wave on each map is kept.

Top Bar & Mob Size

In Map 1 and the Map Editor a bar runs along the top of the screen: GayZ, the game mode you're playing (or Map Editor / Try Map), your FPS, MS (how long each frame takes), your coordinates, then your device's chip and how many CPU cores it has, the weather and the time of day (to the minute - a game minute goes by every second, so a whole game day passes in under half an hour). It's made of the ground of the biome you're in - leaves in forests, sand in deserts, snow, water at sea, stone bricks in the city - and fades into the next one as you walk into it, darker at night and tinted by the weather. The wave, zombies left, kills and coins sit in a line just under it in the same look, changing with the biome too, and the bar stays in view behind the pause screen. While you play, every other game screen wears the biome too, instead of your menu theme - the pause screen, Choose Your Weapon, the inventory, You Died, Settings opened from the pause screen, the corner map and its biome name and the spawn map - filled with that biome's own colour and written in the opposite colour so it's always easy to read (dark boxes with light writing, or light boxes with dark writing); the homepage always keeps your theme. Settings > Theme > Game Screens Follow the Biome turns this off, and then games keep your theme too. Turning off Settings > General > Show FPS Counter During Gameplay hides the FPS, MS and chip parts. Zombies, skeletons, hunched ghouls, brutes, bloaters, skinny runners, your own body in the third-person views, other players and the camp's traders are all 2 blocks tall (slime blobs, zombie dogs and crawlers don't stand up, so they're lower).

Breaking Blocks

While playing, hold the fire button on any block close to you and it cracks on every side you can see (on the bumpy levels too), then breaks - a different crack pattern each time, spreading as it gets closer to breaking, and the block bursts apart its own way. Each block takes its own time: leaves, glass and flowers go almost at once (a broken leaf block always drops a leaf block - 100% of the time), dirt, sand and wool quickly, wood in about a second, stone and bricks longer, ores longer still, and obsidian and bedrock take a long time. Doors, slabs and stairs break like what they're made of. The game's own blocks (chests, spawners, the Player Start) can't be broken, and everything you break is back next game.

Graphics Levels

Settings > Graphics starts with seven picture cards, from light to realistic: Potato (the lightest of all - plain blocks, short view, far fewer pixels shown as crisp squares, and half the rain and snow, for very old or weak devices), Simple (detailed blocks, short view, with richer colour and soft shade in corners and clean, clear water - smoothest on weak phones), Balanced (detailed blocks, medium view, leaves swaying in the wind, smoother hills and water that mirrors the sky), Detailed (detailed, bumpy blocks - brick gaps, rough stone - far view, shadows, smoother hills and little 3D grass plants), Realistic (soft shadows that follow you everywhere, warm sunlight with cool blue shade, natural-looking textures, the sun glinting off stone and off wet ground and puddles after rain, gentle waves, more grass plants, and far land fading into the sky like real air when the weather turns - clear days stay sharp), Ultra (all of that as far as you can see, with softer shadows, deeper corners, stronger glints, rolling waves on the water and dust in the sunlight) and In Real Life (as real as it gets: every block's picture twice as detailed again and like a real surface instead of pixels - rough, gritty rock with hairline cracks, granite with pink, white and black crystals, speckled diorite, sand made of grains, crumbly soil with tiny stones, gravel of round pebbles, deep furrows in tree bark and grain lines in planks - the softest shadows, the deepest shade in corners and under overhangs, the ground's colour changing in soft patches like real grass, dirt and rock, camera-like light and colour where bright spots roll off softly instead of turning flat white, shining puddles after rain, water that mirrors the sky most, bigger waves, trees swaying more and the most dust floating in the sunlight on clear days; when the game slows down it never draws fewer pixels than your screen has - for strong devices, and never picked for you). From Simple up, water is clean and clear: one smooth body of blue water you can see the bottom through, with small rolling ripples that the sun glints off and that mirror the sky - no stripes or square seams between blocks. Every block's picture has soft lighter and darker patches like real ground and stone. From Balanced up the land is less blocky too: the top of every block is gently bumpy (never steeper than 20 degrees) while its edges stay at the block's own height, so you can always see each block and every step - where to jump and where to dig - block edges look rounded, little lumpy rocks lie about on the ground and every leaf block puffs out round and bumpy, so trees look rounded from every side; you still walk, dig and build on whole blocks. Grass plants really grow: a grass block starts bare, and over the next few minutes little sprouts come up one by one and grow tall until, about 3 minutes after you first see it (or place it), it's covered - as far as the land is drawn, with no edge where the grass stops, and nothing in the world hangs in the air: every tree trunk, branch and leaf, and every bell and flag, is held up by something under it. On every level but Potato, real leaves now and then come loose from the trees and flutter down, lie on the ground a moment and fade away - each tree its own shape: five-pointed maple leaves from ordinary trees (some already turned red, orange or gold), toothed birch leaves, pink cherry blossom petals, little spruce twigs of needles, long jungle leaves with a drip tip, feathery acacia leaves, and mangrove, azalea and bamboo leaves. Each level up adds something you can see, and the realistic look is worked out in a lighter way than before, so the top levels run a little smoother. The first time you play, the game looks at your device - its graphics chip, how many CPU cores and how much memory it has, and whether it's a phone - and picks the level that fits, marked "Best for your device"; after that it only changes when you pick another card - or, if the game picked it, when a game keeps running under 30 frames a second even after Auto Quality has nothing left to give up: then it steps down a level, with a note saying so. A level also sets View Distance and Shadows further down, which you can still change on their own, and it switches straight away, even in the middle of a game.

Play Offline

No internet? No problem. After you've opened GayZ online once, the whole game is kept on your device and plays with no connection: Play (Zombie Survival and every Game Mode), the Map Editor and your maps, Design a Skin, Inventory, the Store, Upgrades, Quests, Achievements, Settings and everything else single player. Things that need other players or the cloud - servers, chat, Friends, Clans, the Market, the Gallery, signing in and Cloud Save - are dimmed while you're offline and tell you they need internet, and the game says when you go offline and when you're back - also on Wi-Fi that has no internet, which the game notices by checking for itself. When you're online again, new updates arrive by themselves.

Inventory

Press Tab in a game (or the bag button on a phone) for your inventory: five armor slots (helmet, chestplate, leggings, boots, shield), a ten-by-ten grid and your hotbar. Your gun is in it (just the one you picked - Switch Weapon swaps it), blocks you break pop out as little spinning blocks that you pick up by walking near them (or by pressing Use next to them, with Auto Pick-Up turned off), and Loot Chests drop health packs, ammo boxes, armor, blocks and now and then a Fishing Rod, a Potion, a Metal Detector or a Jetpack (and Snowballs in winter). Pause Menu & Saved Games

Spectate flies a free camera around (on a server it can also look through other players' eyes - click or press Use to switch), with nothing over the view, and Escape comes back to where you were. Switch Weapon opens the weapon picker in the middle of a game, and each gun keeps the ammo it had left. Spectate lets you fly around as yourself while the game keeps going - the land loads where you fly, nothing can hurt you, zombies can't see the camera (they go for the spot you flew off from), the very bottom of the world is a bedrock floor you can't fly under, and everything else works like playing: reload, Use, inspect, the map key, aiming, shooting and breaking blocks. Photo Mode is the same free camera with every bar and button hidden and no gun in your hands: playing alone the whole world freezes for the shot (on a server it keeps going), and a click, P, Enter or the round shutter button saves a picture to your device, with a white flash and a "Photo saved!" note. On a phone the move stick, the up and down buttons, the shutter and an Exit button stay on screen, and the filters sit along the top. Playing alone, dying plays a slow-motion replay of your last 3 seconds first: the buttons and bars go, black bars slide in, the camera circles you while the zombies close in, and your death joke comes up at the end - click, tap, Space, Enter or Escape to skip it. The You Died card also tells you a silly story about the zombie that got you - who it was and the job it had before (24 of them, from accountant to mall Santa) - or a joke if nothing did.

Auto Quality & FPS Cap

Auto Quality (on by default) first draws a little less far away when the game slows down, and only then makes the picture less sharp (on In Real Life never below your screen's own pixels) - sharpness comes back first when it can - and if a game is still too slow after that, a graphics level the game picked steps down one (see Graphics Levels); an FPS Cap setting limits the frame rate. Both are in Settings > Graphics. Behind the scenes the game is built to stay smooth on slower devices: weapon pictures come ready-made, blocks are found quickly, new land is built with little work, zombies and skeletons are prepared before they appear, and the homepage's animations rest while you play. On a slow device new land is built in smaller pieces each frame, so walking into it stutters less, and moving runs in steady steps, so you walk, jump and fall at full speed even at a low frame rate instead of in slow motion.

View Distance

Settings > Graphics > View Distance (Auto, Short, Medium, Far, Max) sets how far away the city is drawn - far blocks stay sharp and clear, and only a thin band of fog at the very edge fades out what's past it, so a shorter one runs much smoother on phones and older computers. Auto starts shorter on a phone, and if the game is still slow at its lowest resolution it draws less on its own.

Lite Textures & Graphics Recovery

Lite Textures (Settings > Graphics) draws the game's big pictures (like the gun models) at half size, so they use far less graphics memory. If your browser drops the game's graphics connection (often from several 3D games open at once), the game pauses with a small note instead of a big error, picks up right where you were when it comes back, and turns on Lite Textures so it's less likely to happen again. To keep memory low, the game lets go of what it doesn't need while you play: land far behind you, extra copies of the world's shapes as soon as they're made, and the homepage's rain and puddles (they come back when you return).
